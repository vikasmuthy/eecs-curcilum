# Drill Bank — Dependent Sources (and Superposition With Them Present)

100 worded problems for 6.002 Topic 03's dependent-source material: VCVS, VCCS, CCVS, CCCS, node analysis with a dependent source's value substituted directly (never as a new unknown), and superposition on circuits where a dependent source stays live through every step. No solutions, no hints, no worked steps.

**Self-verification method for this topic:** after solving for every node voltage, recompute every branch current (via Ohm's law and, for the dependent source's own branch, via its defining relation $v_o=\mu v_c$ / $i_o=g_mv_c$ / $v_o=r_mi_c$ / $i_o=\beta i_c$) and confirm KCL balances at *every* node, including the ones the dependent source touches. If you introduced the dependent source's output as a fresh unknown instead of substituting its algebraic definition, your system is under-determined — that's the single most common way this topic goes wrong, and the balance check will not close until you fix it.

Scope note: every problem is solvable with node analysis (KCL + Ohm's law), one or more dependent sources substituted algebraically, and — where stated — superposition with independent sources. Nothing from Thevenin/Norton is needed here (that's a separate drill bank).

## Batch 1 (1–25) — Single dependent source, one or two nodes

1. A VCCS with $g_m=0.4\text{ S}$ pulls a current $g_m v_1$ from node 1 to ground, controlled by node 1's own voltage. Node 1 is fed by a $9\text{ V}$ source through $R_1=3\ \Omega$, and has $R_2=6\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$.
2. A CCCS with $\beta=8$ draws a current $\beta i_c$ from node 1 to ground, where $i_c$ is the current through $R_1=2\ \Omega$ (connecting a $6\text{ V}$ source to node 1). Node 1 also has $R_2=4\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$ and $i_c$.
3. A CCVS with $r_m=20\ \Omega$ sits in series between node 1 and node 2, producing a voltage $r_mi_c$, where $i_c$ is the current through a separate resistor $R_1=5\ \Omega$ from node 1 to ground. Node 2 connects to ground through $R_2=10\ \Omega$. A $4\text{ A}$ independent source feeds node 1. Find $v_1$ and $v_2$.
4. A VCVS with $\mu=5$ sits in series between node 1 and node 2, its value locked to $v_1$ itself. Node 1 is fed by a $2\text{ mA}$ source and has $R_1=1\ \text{k}\Omega$ to ground; node 2 connects to ground through $R_2=2\ \text{k}\Omega$. Find $v_1$ and $v_2$.
5. A VCCS with $g_m=0.1\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_2$ (the voltage at node 2), injects a current $g_mv_2$ into node 1. Node 1 has $R_1=10\ \Omega$ to ground and is fed by a $3\text{ A}$ source; node 2 is fed by its own $1\text{ A}$ source and has $R_2=5\ \Omega$ to ground (no other connection between the two nodes). Find $v_1$ and $v_2$.
6. A CCCS with $\beta=4$ injects $\beta i_c$ into node 1, where $i_c$ flows through $R_1=10\ \Omega$ from a $5\text{ V}$ source into node 2 (a separate node, not node 1). Node 1 has $R_2=20\ \Omega$ to ground; node 2 has $R_3=10\ \Omega$ to ground in addition to $R_1$. Find $v_1$ and $v_2$.
7. A VCVS with $\mu=2$, controlled by $v_2$, is placed in series with $R_1=6\ \Omega$ between node 1 and ground (i.e., node 1's voltage is forced by $R_1$ and the VCVS in series, fed from ground upward). Node 2 is a separate node fed by a $2\text{ A}$ source with $R_2=3\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_2$ first, then $v_1$.
8. A VCCS with $g_m=1\text{ mS}$ (i.e. $0.001\text{ S}$) pulls $g_mv_1$ from node 1, which is fed by a $12\text{ V}$ source through $R_1=1\ \text{k}\Omega$ and has $R_2=2\ \text{k}\Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$.
9. A CCVS with $r_m=15\ \Omega$, controlled by the current through $R_1=3\ \Omega$ (from node 1 to ground), sits in series with $R_2=9\ \Omega$ between node 1 and an independent $10\text{ V}$ source. Find $v_1$.
10. A CCCS with $\beta=20$, controlled by $i_c$ through $R_1=1\ \text{k}\Omega$ from a $5\text{ V}$ source into node 1, injects $\beta i_c$ into node 1. Node 1 has $R_2=500\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$.
11. Two resistors, $R_1=4\ \Omega$ and $R_2=4\ \Omega$, both connect node 1 to ground (in parallel). A VCCS with $g_m=0.25\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$, also pulls current from node 1 to ground. A $2\text{ A}$ independent source feeds node 1. Find $v_1$.
12. A VCVS with $\mu=10$, controlled by $v_1$, is in series with $R_2=100\ \Omega$ between node 1 and node 2. Node 1 is fed by a $1\text{ mA}$ source with $R_1=1\ \text{k}\Omega$ to ground; node 2 connects to ground through $R_3=1\ \text{k}\Omega$. Find $v_1$ and $v_2$.
13. A CCCS with $\beta=6$, controlled by the current through $R_1=2\ \Omega$ (from a $4\text{ V}$ source to node 1), feeds current into node 1 in parallel with $R_2=8\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$.
14. A VCCS with $g_m=2\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$, pulls current from node 1; node 1 has only one resistor, $R_1=0.5\ \Omega$, to ground, and is fed by a $1\text{ A}$ independent source. Find $v_1$ (watch the units).
15. A CCVS with $r_m=4\ \Omega$, controlled by current through $R_1=2\ \Omega$ from node 1 to ground, is placed in series with $R_2=6\ \Omega$ between node 1 and a $20\text{ V}$ source. Find $v_1$.
16. A VCVS with $\mu=1$ (unity gain), controlled by $v_1$, is placed in series between node 1 and node 2, with $R_2=50\ \Omega$ from node 2 to ground. Node 1 has $R_1=100\ \Omega$ to ground and a $5\text{ mA}$ source. Find $v_1$ and $v_2$, and explain what a unity-gain VCVS effectively does to node 2's relationship to node 1.
17. A VCCS with $g_m=0.05\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$, injects (not pulls) current into node 1. Node 1 has $R_1=20\ \Omega$ to ground and is fed by a $2\text{ A}$ source. Find $v_1$ (note the sign difference from a VCCS that *pulls* current — write the correct KCL sign).
18. A CCCS with $\beta=15$, controlled by current through $R_1=4\ \Omega$ (from a $8\text{ V}$ source into node 1), pulls current from node 1. Node 1 has $R_2=8\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$.
19. A VCVS with $\mu=3$, controlled by $v_2$, is in series with $R_1=10\ \Omega$ feeding node 1 from ground; node 2 is fed independently by a $6\text{ A}$ source with $R_2=2\ \Omega$ to ground; node 1 additionally has $R_3=20\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_2$ then $v_1$.
20. A CCVS with $r_m=8\ \Omega$, controlled by current through $R_1=1\ \Omega$ from node 1 to ground, is in series with $R_2=3\ \Omega$ between node 1 and a $9\text{ V}$ source. Find $v_1$.
21. A VCCS with $g_m=0.3\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$, pulls current from node 1, which also has two resistors to ground in parallel: $R_1=10\ \Omega$ and $R_2=10\ \Omega$. A $5\text{ A}$ source feeds node 1. Find $v_1$.
22. A CCCS with $\beta=3$, controlled by current through $R_1=6\ \Omega$ (from a $12\text{ V}$ source to node 1), pulls current from node 1, which has $R_2=6\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$ and $i_c$, and verify $i_c$ satisfies the branch's own Ohm's-law relation once $v_1$ is known.
23. A VCVS with $\mu=4$, controlled by $v_1$, is placed directly from node 1 to node 2 (in parallel with nothing else between those two specific nodes — it's the only connection between them). Node 1 has $R_1=5\ \Omega$ to ground and a $2\text{ A}$ source; node 2 has $R_2=15\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$ and $v_2$.
24. A VCCS with $g_m=0.02\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$, pulls current from node 1. Node 1 is fed through $R_1=5\ \text{k}\Omega$ from a $15\text{ V}$ source, and has $R_2=10\ \text{k}\Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$.
25. A CCVS with $r_m=12\ \Omega$, controlled by the current through $R_1=4\ \Omega$ from a $16\text{ V}$ source to node 1, is placed in series with $R_2=4\ \Omega$ between node 1 and ground (i.e., node 1 connects to ground only through this series combination). Find $v_1$.

## Batch 3 (51–75) — Sign/pitfall traps and superposition with a dependent source present

51. A student solving a circuit with a VCCS writes a new unknown $i_x$ for its output current instead of substituting $g_mv_1$ directly, then wonders why they have one more unknown than equations. Given $g_m=0.2\text{ S}$, node 1 fed by a $4\text{ A}$ source with $R_1=5\ \Omega$ to ground and the VCCS pulling current from node 1, solve correctly (substituting directly) and explain what went wrong with the student's approach.
52. A circuit has one independent source ($8\text{ V}$ through $R_1=4\ \Omega$ into node 1) and a VCCS ($g_m=0.25\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$, pulling current from node 1 to ground) alongside $R_2=4\ \Omega$ to ground. A student, applying superposition, zeros the VCCS (treats it as an open circuit) when isolating the independent source's contribution. Explain why this is wrong, then solve the circuit correctly using direct node analysis (not flawed superposition) for comparison.
53. A CCVS's controlling current $i_c$ is defined flowing through $R_1=5\ \Omega$ in one direction, but a student writes the CCVS's value using the current in the opposite direction (a sign flip). Given $r_m=10\ \Omega$ and the correct $i_c=2\text{ A}$ (in the defined direction), state what value the student's sign-flipped version would produce instead, and explain the physical consequence of using the wrong sign.
54. A VCCS has $g_m$ given in units of $\text{mA/V}$ (a common datasheet unit) as $g_m=5\text{ mA/V}$, but a student plugs this number directly into a formula expecting siemens (A/V) without converting. Given a controlling voltage $v_c=2\text{ V}$, compute the VCCS's output current correctly (with proper unit conversion) and state what the student's unconverted number would have implied instead.
55. A circuit has two independent sources (a $10\text{ V}$ source and a $2\text{ A}$ source) and one VCCS ($g_m=0.1\text{ S}$, controlled by node 1's voltage, pulling current from node 1). 56. 60. 73. A network has a VCCS ($g_m=0.2\text{ S}$) whose controlling voltage $v_c$ is defined as $v_1-v_2$ (a voltage *difference* between two nodes, not a single node's voltage relative to ground). Node 1 is fed by a $4\text{ A}$ source with $R_1=5\ \Omega$ to ground; node 2 is fed by a $1\text{ A}$ source with $R_2=5\ \Omega$ to ground; the VCCS pulls $g_m(v_1-v_2)$ from node 1 to node 2. Solve for $v_1$ and $v_2$, being careful with how a difference-controlled source enters both nodes' KCL equations (with opposite signs).
74. Explain why, in the note's node-analysis procedure, a dependent source never changes the *number* of equations or unknowns in the system compared to solving the same circuit with that branch simply absent — using the specific example of a VCCS with $g_m=0.3\text{ S}$ controlled by $v_1$, in a circuit otherwise identical to Problem 1 of Batch 1, to illustrate concretely what "the same system size" means.
75. A circuit has a dependent source and a single independent source, and a student, out of habit from single-source circuits, doesn't bother with superposition at all (correctly, since superposition is only useful/needed with *multiple* independent sources) but then second-guesses themselves, wondering if they "still need to zero the dependent source somehow" even in this single-independent-source case. Given $g_m=0.1\text{ S}$ (VCCS controlled by $v_1$), one $5\text{ A}$ independent source feeding node 1, and $R_1=10\ \Omega$ to ground, resolve the student's confusion and solve directly.

## Batch 4 (76–100) — Harder / multi-node / multi-dependent-source networks

76. A circuit has two dependent sources: a VCCS ($g_m=0.2\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$) pulling current from node 1, and a CCVS ($r_m=10\ \Omega$, controlled by the current through $R_2=5\ \Omega$ from node 1 to node 2) in series between node 2 and ground. Node 1 is fed by a $6\text{ A}$ source with $R_1=5\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$ and $v_2$.
77. A three-node network has a VCVS ($\mu=3$, controlled by $v_1$) between nodes 1 and 2, a CCCS ($\beta=4$, controlled by the current through $R_3=2\ \Omega$ from node 2 to node 3) pulling current from node 3, and independent sources: $2\text{ A}$ into node 1 (with $R_1=4\ \Omega$ to ground) and $R_4=6\ \Omega$ from node 3 to ground. Find $v_1$, $v_2$, $v_3$.
78. A network has two VCCS's: one ($g_{m1}=0.1\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$) pulling current from node 1, and another ($g_{m2}=0.2\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_2$) pulling current from node 2. Node 1 and node 2 are connected by $R_{12}=5\ \Omega$; node 1 is fed by a $4\text{ A}$ source with $R_1=10\ \Omega$ to ground; node 2 has $R_2=10\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$ and $v_2$.
79. A cascaded amplifier model has stage 1: a VCCS ($g_m=0.05\text{ S}$, controlled by an input $v_{in}=0.2\text{ V}$ fixed independently) pulling current from node 1, which has $R_1=2\ \text{k}\Omega$ to a supply, giving $v_1$. Stage 2: a second VCCS ($g_m=0.05\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$ from stage 1) pulling current from node 2, which has $R_2=2\ \text{k}\Omega$ to the same supply, giving the final output $v_2$. Treat the supply as a fixed $10\text{ V}$ node. Find $v_1$ and $v_2$.
80. A network has one CCVS ($r_m=5\ \Omega$, controlled by current $i_1$ through $R_1=1\ \Omega$ from a $10\text{ V}$ source into node 1) in series with $R_2=3\ \Omega$ between node 1 and node 2, and a second CCVS ($r_m=5\ \Omega$ also, controlled by current $i_2$ through $R_3=1\ \Omega$ from node 2 to ground) in series with $R_4=3\ \Omega$ between node 2 and ground. Find $v_1$, $v_2$, $i_1$, $i_2$ (this one requires setting up all four unknowns together).
81. A bridge-like network with a VCCS: $R_1=4\ \Omega$ connects node 1 to node 2; $R_2=4\ \Omega$ connects node 1 to node 3; a VCCS ($g_m=0.25\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_2-v_3$) connects node 2 to node 3 (pulling current from 2 to 3 proportional to their difference); $R_3=8\ \Omega$ connects node 2 to ground; $R_4=8\ \Omega$ connects node 3 to ground; a $2\text{ A}$ source feeds node 1. Find $v_1, v_2, v_3$.
82. Superposition with two independent sources and two dependent sources present throughout: a $12\text{ V}$ source and a $3\text{ A}$ source both feed node 1 (through $R_1=4\ \Omega$ and directly, respectively); node 1 has $R_2=4\ \Omega$ to ground; a VCCS ($g_m=0.1\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$) also pulls current from node 1; a second, separate VCVS ($\mu=2$, controlled by $v_1$) drives node 2 through $R_3=10\ \Omega$, with $R_4=10\ \Omega$ from node 2 to ground. Using superposition (both dependent sources present at every step), find $v_1$ and $v_2$ as sums of two sub-problems.
83. A network has a VCCS ($g_m=0.4\text{ S}$) whose controlling voltage is $v_3$ (a third node not directly connected to node 1 by any resistor at all — only through the dependent source's action does node 3's voltage affect node 1). Node 1 is fed by a $5\text{ A}$ source with $R_1=5\ \Omega$ to ground, and the VCCS pulls current from node 1 to ground controlled by $v_3$; node 3 is fed independently by a $2\text{ A}$ source with $R_3=4\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$ and $v_3$, and explain why solving for $v_3$ first (even though it's "unrelated" by direct wiring to node 1) is necessary before $v_1$ can be found.
84. A four-node network with one CCCS: $R_1=2\ \Omega$ from a $20\text{ V}$ source into node 1; $R_2=4\ \Omega$ from node 1 to node 2; $R_3=6\ \Omega$ from node 2 to ground; a CCCS ($\beta=3$, controlled by the current through $R_2$) pulls current from node 2 to node 3; $R_4=8\ \Omega$ from node 3 to ground. Find $v_1$, $v_2$, $v_3$.
85. A network has a VCVS in a feedback-like arrangement: $R_1=10\ \Omega$ from a $5\text{ A}$ source into node 1; a VCVS ($\mu=8$, controlled by $v_1$) in series with $R_2=20\ \Omega$ feeding back into node 1 itself from ground (i.e., the VCVS+$R_2$ branch connects node 1 to ground, with the VCVS's own value depending on that same node 1's voltage). Find $v_1$.
86. Two CCCS's share a controlling branch: $R_1=5\ \Omega$ carries current $i_c$ from a $15\text{ V}$ source into node 1; a first CCCS ($\beta_1=2$, controlled by $i_c$) pulls current from node 1; a second CCCS ($\beta_2=3$, also controlled by the *same* $i_c$) pulls current from a separate node 2, which has $R_2=6\ \Omega$ to ground; node 1 has $R_3=6\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$, $v_2$, and $i_c$.
87. A network has a dependent source whose controlling variable depends on a node that is itself set by a different dependent source (a genuine dependency chain): node 1 is fed by a $3\text{ A}$ source with $R_1=4\ \Omega$ to ground; a VCCS ($g_m=0.5\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$) feeds current into node 2, which has $R_2=2\ \Omega$ to ground, giving $v_2$; a CCVS ($r_m=4\ \Omega$, controlled by the current through $R_2$) then feeds, in series with $R_3=6\ \Omega$, into node 3, which has $R_4=10\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$, $v_2$, and $v_3$ in that order.
88. A network with symmetric dependent sources: two identical VCCS's ($g_m=0.2\text{ S}$ each), one controlled by $v_1$ pulling current from node 2, the other controlled by $v_2$ pulling current from node 1 (a cross-coupled pair). Node 1 is fed by a $4\text{ A}$ source with $R_1=5\ \Omega$ to ground; node 2 is fed by a $4\text{ A}$ source with $R_2=5\ \Omega$ to ground (symmetric sources too). By symmetry, guess a relationship between $v_1$ and $v_2$ before solving algebraically, then solve the full $2\times2$ system and confirm your symmetry guess.
89. A network has a VCVS with $\mu=0$ (a degenerate case — effectively the VCVS's output is forced to exactly $0\text{ V}$ regardless of its controlling variable). Explain what this reduces to physically (compare it to just a plain wire, i.e. a short), then solve a circuit where this "VCVS" sits between node 1 and ground, with node 1 also fed by $R_1=10\ \Omega$ from a $6\text{ V}$ source, for $v_1$.
90. A network has a CCCS with $\beta=0$. Explain what this reduces to physically (compare it to an open circuit, i.e. no branch at all), then solve a circuit where this "CCCS" was supposed to feed node 1 (which also has $R_1=5\ \Omega$ to ground and a $3\text{ A}$ independent source), for $v_1$, confirming the CCCS contributes nothing to the answer.
91. A five-resistor, two-dependent-source network: $R_1=2\ \Omega$ from a $10\text{ A}$ source into node 1; $R_2=4\ \Omega$ from node 1 to ground; a VCCS ($g_m=0.3\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$) pulls current from node 1 to node 2; $R_3=5\ \Omega$ from node 2 to ground; a CCVS ($r_m=2\ \Omega$, controlled by current through $R_3$) in series with $R_4=3\ \Omega$ feeds from node 2 into node 3, which has $R_5=10\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$, $v_2$, $v_3$.
92. A network's dependent source has a controlling variable defined through a supernode-like arrangement: two nodes, 1 and 2, are joined by an independent $6\text{ V}$ source directly (a floating source, forcing $v_1-v_2=6$); a VCCS ($g_m=0.2\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$) pulls current from a third node, node 3, which has $R_3=5\ \Omega$ to ground; node 1 has $R_1=10\ \Omega$ to ground and node 2 has $R_2=10\ \Omega$ to ground (with a combined/supernode KCL equation needed for nodes 1 and 2 together). Find $v_1$, $v_2$, $v_3$.
93. A network has a VCCS controlled by a voltage that is itself the output of a voltage divider fed by an independent source, all within the same one-port: a $9\text{ V}$ source feeds a divider of $R_1=3\ \Omega$ and $R_2=6\ \Omega$ to ground (defining an intermediate node voltage $v_x$), and a VCCS ($g_m=0.4\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_x$) pulls current from a separate output node with $R_3=4\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_x$ and the output node's voltage.
94. Superposition with three independent sources and one dependent source: node 1 is fed by a $9\text{ V}$ source through $R_1=3\ \Omega$, a $2\text{ A}$ source directly, and a second $1\text{ A}$ source directly (three independent sources total, all at node 1); node 1 also has $R_2=6\ \Omega$ to ground and a VCCS ($g_m=0.1\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$) pulling current from node 1 throughout. Solve using superposition (three sub-problems, dependent source active in each) and verify the sum against a single direct node-analysis solve.
95. A network has a dependent source whose gain is itself given as an expression rather than a plain number: a VCCS with $g_m = 1/R_5$ where $R_5=20\ \Omega$ is a separate resistor elsewhere in the circuit used only to set this gain (not carrying any current itself in this idealized problem — just treat $g_m=1/20\text{ S}=0.05\text{ S}$ once computed). Controlled by $v_1$, pulling current from node 1, which is fed by a $2\text{ A}$ source with $R_1=10\ \Omega$ to ground. Find $v_1$.
96. A network has a CCVS in series with a VCVS (two dependent sources in the same branch): a $r_m=5\ \Omega$ CCVS (controlled by current through $R_1=2\ \Omega$ from a $10\text{ V}$ source into node 1) is in series with a $\mu=2$ VCVS (controlled by $v_1$ itself) and then $R_2=3\ \Omega$, all connecting node 1 to ground. Find $v_1$.
97. A network requires recognizing that a dependent source's controlling branch carries zero current in the final solution (not because the branch is open, but because the numbers happen to work out that way): a CCCS ($\beta=10$) is controlled by current through $R_1=5\ \Omega$ between node 1 and node 2, both of which are fed such that (after solving) $v_1=v_2$ exactly, making $i_c=0$. Given node 1 is fed by a $4\text{ A}$ source with $R_3=5\ \Omega$ to ground and node 2 is fed by a separately-tuned source such that this equality holds, confirm $i_c=0$ follows and find what the CCCS then contributes to wherever it feeds.
98. A network has a VCCS whose output current, once computed, comes out negative (i.e., it's actually pulling current in the opposite direction from how it was defined). Given $g_m=0.2\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$, and $v_1$ solved to be $-3\text{ V}$ from the rest of the circuit's equations, state the VCCS's actual output current and explain (in terms of the controlling variable's own sign) why a negative controlling variable is not itself an error.
99. A large network combines everything: two independent sources, two VCCS's, and one CCVS, across four nodes. (a) $8\text{ A}$ source feeds node 1, $R_1=4\ \Omega$ from node 1 to ground; (b) VCCS$_1$ ($g_m=0.2\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_1$) pulls current from node 1 to node 2; (c) $R_2=5\ \Omega$ from node 2 to ground; (d) VCCS$_2$ ($g_m=0.1\text{ S}$, controlled by $v_2$) pulls current from node 2 to node 3; (e) $R_3=10\ \Omega$ from node 3 to ground; (f) CCVS ($r_m=4\ \Omega$, controlled by current through $R_3$) in series with $R_4=6\ \Omega$ feeds from node 3 into node 4; (g) $R_5=20\ \Omega$ from node 4 to ground; (h) an independent $6\text{ V}$ source is in series with $R_4$ as well (aiding or opposing the CCVS — state your assumed polarity). Find all four node voltages.
100. Explain, in general terms, why no matter how many dependent sources a linear resistive circuit contains (chained, cross-coupled, or nested as in Problems 76–99), the node-analysis system never gains extra unknowns or requires anything beyond KCL, Ohm's law, and direct substitution of each dependent source's defining relation — and why this guarantee is exactly what makes "just keep substituting and solving the linear system" a complete strategy, no matter how tangled the dependency structure looks at first glance.
