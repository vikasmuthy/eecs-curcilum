# Drill Bank — Equivalent Resistance (Series/Parallel Combination Only)

100 worded problems for 6.002 Topic 02's series/parallel combination material specifically — not node analysis, not mesh analysis, not full circuit solving. Every problem here asks for exactly one thing: **the equivalent resistance $R_{eq}$** seen at two stated terminals of a resistor-only network. No solutions, no hints, no worked steps.

**Self-verification method for this topic (not an answer key):** pick a convenient hypothetical voltage across the two terminals you're asked about (any value — $10\text{ V}$, $100\text{ V}$, whatever's arithmetically clean), compute the total current your answer predicts ($i=v/R_{eq}$), then walk back through the actual network branch by branch using Ohm's law and confirm every intermediate KCL junction balances (current in = current out) — exactly the "Check, from first principles" step in the note's Example 1. If it doesn't balance, your $R_{eq}$ is wrong regardless of how confident the algebra felt.

Scope note: every network below is resistors only — no sources, no node voltages to solve for, nothing beyond what Topic 02's series/parallel derivation established. If a network (or part of one) isn't reducible by series/parallel at all, the problem will say so explicitly and ask you to identify why, rather than asking for a numeric $R_{eq}$ that doesn't exist by this method.

## Batch 1 (1–25) — Pure series and pure parallel

1. Three resistors, $100\ \Omega$, $220\ \Omega$, and $330\ \Omega$, are connected end to end with nothing else attached to the two junctions between them. Find $R_{eq}$ across the outer two terminals.
2. Two resistors, $47\ \Omega$ and $53\ \Omega$, are the only two branches between the same pair of terminals. Find $R_{eq}$.
3. Four equal resistors of $25\ \Omega$ each are wired in series. Find $R_{eq}$.
4. Four equal resistors of $60\ \Omega$ each are wired in parallel (all four sharing the same two nodes). Find $R_{eq}$.
5. Two resistors, $1\ \text{k}\Omega$ and $4\ \text{k}\Omega$, share the same two terminals (nothing else connects there). Find $R_{eq}$.
6. Five resistors of $10\ \Omega$ each are wired in series. Find $R_eq$ and state, in general, the pattern for $n$ equal resistors of value $R$ in series.
7. Three resistors, $6\ \Omega$, $12\ \Omega$, $4\ \Omega$, all share the same two terminals (all three in parallel). Find $R_{eq}$.
8. Two resistors, $15\ \Omega$ and $15\ \Omega$ (equal), are in series. Find $R_{eq}$ and explain, without recomputing, why doubling one resistor's value while halving the other would NOT give the same $R_{eq}$.
9. Two equal resistors, $R$ each, are in parallel. Prove (using the general parallel formula, not just a numeric example) that $R_{eq}=R/2$ regardless of $R$'s specific value.
10. Six resistors, all $18\ \Omega$, are wired in parallel. Find $R_{eq}$.
11. Two resistors, $2.2\ \text{k}\Omega$ and $6.8\ \text{k}\Omega$, are in series. Find $R_{eq}$.
12. Three resistors, $50\ \Omega$, $100\ \Omega$, $200\ \Omega$, are in parallel. Find $R_{eq}$.
13. A single resistor of $75\ \Omega$ is, by itself, the only branch between two terminals. State $R_{eq}$ and explain why the series/parallel formulas trivially agree on this case.
14. Two resistors, $R_1$ and $R_2$, are in series, and their combined $R_{eq}$ is measured at $500\ \Omega$. If $R_1=200\ \Omega$, find $R_2$.
15. Two resistors are in parallel with combined $R_{eq}=6\ \Omega$. If one resistor is $10\ \Omega$, find the other.
16. Seven identical resistors of $R=140\ \Omega$ are wired in series. Find $R_{eq}$.
17. Two resistors, $330\ \Omega$ and $470\ \Omega$, are in parallel. Find $R_{eq}$ (round to the nearest ohm).
18. Three resistors, $R_1=10\ \Omega$, $R_2=20\ \Omega$, $R_3=30\ \Omega$, are all in parallel. Find $R_{eq}$ and confirm it is smaller than the smallest individual resistor — explain, in one sentence, why parallel combination always makes $R_{eq}$ smaller than every individual branch.
19. Two resistors, one $R$ and one $9R$ (in terms of an unspecified $R$), are in series. Express $R_{eq}$ in terms of $R$.
20. Two resistors, one $R$ and one $9R$, are instead in parallel. Express $R_{eq}$ in terms of $R$ (simplify to a single fraction of $R$).
21. Ten identical resistors of $1\ \text{k}\Omega$ each are wired in parallel. Find $R_{eq}$.
22. A network has $R_1=40\ \Omega$ in series with $R_2=60\ \Omega}$, and this series pair is itself treated as one branch; a second, separate branch of $R_3=100\ \Omega$ is in parallel with that whole series pair. Find $R_{eq}$.
23. Two resistors, $8\ \Omega$ and $8\ \Omega$ (equal), are in parallel; separately, two more resistors, $8\ \Omega$ and $8\ \Omega$, are also in parallel with each other, and these two parallel-pairs are then placed in series with each other. Find the overall $R_{eq}$.
24. A resistor of $R=0\ \Omega$ (an ideal wire) is placed in parallel with a resistor $R_2=500\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$ and explain physically why this result makes sense (what happens to current in the $500\ \Omega$ branch).
25. A resistor of $R\to\infty$ (an open circuit, i.e. no connection at all) is placed in parallel with $R_2=750\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$ and explain why an open branch never changes a parallel combination's equivalent resistance.

## Batch 2 (26–50) — Series-parallel combinations (multi-step reduction)

26. $R_1=10\ \Omega$ is in series with the parallel combination of $R_2=20\ \Omega$ and $R_3=20\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
27. $R_1=100\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_2=100\ \Omega$, and that whole combination in series with $R_3=50\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
28. Three resistors: $R_1=5\ \Omega$ in series with $R_2=5\ \Omega$; that series pair is in parallel with a third resistor $R_3=10\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
29. $R_1=12\ \Omega$, $R_2=6\ \Omega$, $R_3=4\ \Omega$: $R_2$ and $R_3$ are in parallel with each other, and that combination is in series with $R_1$. Find $R_{eq}$.
30. Four resistors: $R_1=20\ \Omega$ in series with $R_2=30\ \Omega$; separately, $R_3=15\ \Omega$ in series with $R_4=35\ \Omega$; these two series chains are then placed in parallel with each other. Find $R_{eq}$.
31. $R_1=8\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_2=24\ \Omega$; that result in series with $R_3=2\ \Omega$; that whole result in parallel with $R_4=15\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
32. $R_1=1\ \text{k}\Omega$ in series with $R_2=1\ \text{k}\Omega$; that pair in parallel with $R_3=2\ \text{k}\Omega$ (note the parallel pair's value cleverly matches $R_3$ — find $R_{eq}$ and see what happens).
33. A network has $R_1=6\ \Omega$ in series with $R_2=3\ \Omega$; the series pair is in parallel with $R_3=9\ \Omega$; the resulting combination is in series with $R_4=1\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
34. Two parallel pairs in series: ($R_1=4\ \Omega \parallel R_2=4\ \Omega$) in series with ($R_3=12\ \Omega \parallel R_4=6\ \Omega$). Find $R_{eq}$.
35. $R_1=18\ \Omega$ in parallel with the series combination of $R_2=9\ \Omega$ and $R_3=9\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
36. Nested combination: $R_1=2\ \Omega$ in series with [$R_2=4\ \Omega$ in parallel with ($R_3=3\ \Omega$ in series with $R_4=1\ \Omega$)]. Find $R_{eq}$.
37. $R_1=50\ \Omega$, $R_2=50\ \Omega$, $R_3=50\ \Omega$: $R_1$ in series with $R_2$, then that pair in parallel with $R_3$. Find $R_{eq}$ and simplify to a clean fraction.
38. A ladder of three stages: $R_1=10\ \Omega$ in series, then $R_2=10\ \Omega$ in parallel (to the return path), then $R_3=10\ \Omega$ in series, then $R_4=10\ \Omega$ in parallel — reduce from the far end inward and find the overall $R_{eq}$ (this is the same style of ladder network used later for digital logic delay estimates).
39. $R_1=100\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_2=25\ \Omega$; separately $R_3=60\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_4=40\ \Omega$; the two parallel results are then in series. Find $R_{eq}$.
40. $R_1=9\ \Omega$, $R_2=18\ \Omega$, $R_3=6\ \Omega$, all three in parallel with each other (not nested — all three share the same two nodes). Find $R_{eq}$.
41. $R_1=7\ \Omega$ in series with $R_2=7\ \Omega$ in series with $R_3=7\ \Omega$; the entire series chain is in parallel with a single resistor $R_4=21\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
42. Two branches in parallel: branch 1 is $R_1=5\ \Omega$ alone; branch 2 is $R_2=5\ \Omega$ in series with $R_3=5\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$ (note the branches are NOT symmetric — don't assume the current splits evenly).
43. $R_1=30\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_2=60\ \Omega$; result in series with $R_3=10\ \Omega$; result in parallel with $R_4=40\ \Omega$; result in series with $R_5=5\ \Omega$. Find the final $R_{eq}$ (five resistors total).
44. A bridge-free network: $R_1=16\ \Omega$ in series with [$R_2=8\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_3=8\ \Omega$] in series with $R_4=4\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
45. Three identical parallel pairs in series: ($10\ \Omega\parallel10\ \Omega$) + ($10\ \Omega\parallel10\ \Omega$) + ($10\ \Omega\parallel10\ \Omega$), all three results placed in series with each other. Find $R_{eq}$.
46. $R_1=45\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_2=90\ \Omega$; result in parallel (again) with $R_3=30\ \Omega$ — i.e., all three effectively in parallel but reduce it two resistors at a time rather than using the three-term formula directly, and confirm you get the same answer either way.
47. $R_1=1\ \Omega$ in series with $R_2=2\ \Omega$ in series with $R_3=3\ \Omega$; the whole chain in parallel with a single $R_4=?$ such that the overall $R_{eq}=3\ \Omega$ exactly. Find $R_4$.
48. $R_1=100\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_2=100\ \Omega$; this pair in series with an identical parallel pair of $R_3=100\ \Omega \parallel R_4=100\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
49. A network has $R_1=20\ \Omega$ in series with $R_2=5\ \Omega$; separately a single resistor $R_3=25\ \Omega$ connects the same two outer terminals directly (in parallel with the whole $R_1$–$R_2$ series chain). Find $R_{eq}$ and note anything convenient about the numbers.
50. $R_1=12\ \Omega$, $R_2=12\ \Omega$, $R_3=12\ \Omega$, $R_4=12\ \Omega$: $R_1\parallel R_2$ in series with $R_3\parallel R_4$. Find $R_{eq}$.

## Batch 3 (51–75) — Real-world framing (translate the description into a network first)

51. A string of four decorative LED-indicator resistors (treated as linear resistors here), each $R=150\ \Omega$, is wired end to end with no branch points in between. Find the equivalent resistance the power supply sees.
52. A car stereo has two speaker outputs, each presenting $R=8\ \Omega$, wired to the same pair of amplifier output terminals (a standard "parallel speaker" wiring). Find the load resistance the amplifier sees.
53. A resistor ladder used to set a reference voltage has three $1\ \text{k}\Omega$ resistors in series between a supply rail and ground, with no other connections at the two internal nodes. Find the total resistance between the rail and ground.
54. A sensor board has its main sensing resistor, $R_1=2\ \text{k}\Omega$, in parallel with a "bleed" resistor $R_2=10\ \text{k}\Omega$ added specifically to guarantee a minimum current path even if the sensor fails open. Find the equivalent resistance presented to the rest of the board.
55. A space heater has two identical heating elements, each $R=24\ \Omega$, that can be wired either in series (low-heat mode) or parallel (high-heat mode) by a mode switch. Find $R_{eq}$ in both modes.
56. A guitar amplifier's speaker cabinet has four $16\ \Omega$ speakers; the cabinet's internal wiring puts two in series to form each of two "sub-groups," and then wires the two sub-groups in parallel with each other. Find the overall cabinet impedance (treated purely resistively for this problem).
57. A pull-up resistor network has $R_1=4.7\ \text{k}\Omega$ from a supply rail to a signal node, and that signal node also connects to ground through the parallel combination of two additional resistors, $R_2=10\ \text{k}\Omega$ and $R_3=10\ \text{k}\Omega$ (representing two input pins tied together). Find the equivalent resistance from the supply rail to ground through this whole path.
58. A holiday light string has 20 identical bulbs (each modeled as $R=5\ \Omega$ for this problem) wired in series. Find the total string resistance, and explain why one bulb burning out (becoming an open circuit) affects the whole string's behavior differently than it would in a parallel-wired string.
59. A power strip's surge-protection network has an MOV (modeled here as a fixed $R=1\ \text{k}\Omega$ resistor for this problem) in parallel with the rest of the strip's internal wiring resistance, $R=0.05\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$ and explain, physically, which resistance dominates the parallel combination and why.
60. A model railway layout has two parallel feeder wires to a section of track, each contributing $R=0.8\ \Omega$ of resistance, feeding a single $R=12\ \Omega$ locomotive load in series after the feeders join. Find the total resistance from the power source to the locomotive.
61. A thermostat's bimetallic sensing element is modeled as $R_1=3\ \text{k}\Omega$ in series with a calibration trim resistor $R_2=500\ \Omega$; this series combination is itself in parallel with a fixed reference resistor $R_3=4\ \text{k}\Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
62. A guitar pedal's tone-shaping network has three resistors, $10\ \text{k}\Omega$, $22\ \text{k}\Omega$, and $47\ \text{k}\Omega$, all wired between the same two nodes (all three in parallel, representing three parallel signal paths). Find $R_{eq}$.
63. An electric blanket has five heating zones, each $R=40\ \Omega$, all wired in parallel to spread the load evenly. Find the equivalent resistance of the whole blanket.
64. A weighing-scale strain gauge bridge has one arm modeled as $R_1=350\ \Omega$ in series with a lead-wire resistance of $R_2=2\ \Omega$; this series combination is in parallel with a matched reference arm of $R_3=352\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$ for this one side of the bridge (not the full bridge — just this parallel pair).
65. A garden lighting transformer feeds a run of cable modeled as $R=0.6\ \Omega$ in series with the first light fixture's resistance $R=20\ \Omega$; a second identical fixture is wired in parallel with the first (after the cable resistance, both fixtures sharing the same two terminals). Find the resistance from the transformer's perspective (cable in series with the parallel pair of fixtures).
66. A battery pack's cell-balancing network has each of two cells' bleed resistors, $R=100\ \Omega$ each, wired in parallel across their respective cells; the problem only asks about one such parallel pair — but there are two identical resistors from a single cell's + to - terminals (redundant bleed paths). Find $R_{eq}$ for that one cell's bleed network.
67. An intercom system's two-wire line has $R=3\ \Omega$ of line resistance in series with the intercom unit's own input resistance of $R=600\ \Omega$. Find the total resistance seen by the source end of the line.
68. A 3D printer's heated bed has four heating resistors, each $R=2\ \Omega$, wired in a 2-series-by-2-parallel arrangement: two pairs, each pair being two resistors in series, and the two pairs then wired in parallel with each other. Find the bed's overall equivalent resistance.
69. A ceiling fan's speed-control network switches in an additional series resistor, $R=15\ \Omega$, ahead of the motor's own resistance, $R=45\ \Omega$, for low speed; for high speed, the added resistor is bypassed entirely (shorted out, i.e. $R=0\ \Omega$ in series). Find $R_{eq}$ in both the low-speed and high-speed configurations.
70. A guitar's passive volume pot, at a specific rotation, splits into two resistive sections, $R_1=7\ \text{k}\Omega$ and $R_2=3\ \text{k}\Omega$, in series between the pickup and ground, with the signal tapped from their junction — the problem here only asks for the total resistance from pickup to ground, i.e. the series sum. Find it.
71. An automotive relay coil, $R=80\ \Omega$, is wired in parallel with a flyback (freewheeling) diode's forward "on" resistance, modeled here as $R=5\ \Omega$ for this problem only (ignore the diode's nonlinearity). Find $R_{eq}$.
72. A test bench has a $50\ \Omega$ reference load in parallel with the device under test, modeled as $R=450\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$ and state what fraction of the total current the $50\ \Omega$ reference load carries for a given applied voltage (you'll need to find each branch current relative to the total to answer the fraction part — but the main task is still just $R_{eq}$).
73. A network of Christmas tree lights has two parallel strings, each string being five $R=8\ \Omega$ bulbs in series. Find the equivalent resistance of the two strings combined.
74. A power-over-Ethernet injector has its sense resistor $R_1=25.5\ \text{k}\Omega$ in series with the cable resistance $R_2=8\ \Omega$ (cable resistance is negligible in comparison, but include it anyway), and this whole series path is in parallel with a bleed resistor $R_3=100\ \text{k}\Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
75. A doorbell transformer's secondary winding resistance ($R=4\ \Omega$) is in series with two parallel-wired bell coils, each $R=16\ \Omega$. Find the total resistance the transformer's secondary sees.

## Batch 4 (76–100) — Harder reductions, larger networks, and where series/parallel stops working

76. Reduce fully: $R_1=2\ \Omega$ in series with [$R_2=3\ \Omega$ parallel with ($R_3=6\ \Omega$ series $R_4=6\ \Omega$)] in series with $R_5=1\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
77. A ladder network of six resistors: starting from the far end, $R_6=10\ \Omega$ terminates the ladder; working back, alternating series ($R_5=5\ \Omega$, $R_3=5\ \Omega$, $R_1=5\ \Omega$) and parallel-to-return-path ($R_4=10\ \Omega$, $R_2=10\ \Omega$) stages. Reduce from the far end inward and find the total $R_{eq}$ (this is the standard R-2R-style ladder structure used later in digital-to-analog conversion, though the values here are illustrative, not an actual R-2R ladder).
78. $R_1=100\ \Omega$, $R_2=200\ \Omega$, $R_3=300\ \Omega$, $R_4=400\ \Omega$, all four in parallel with each other. Find $R_{eq}$ (exact fraction, then decimal).
79. A network has three parallel branches between the same two terminals: branch 1 is $R_1=6\ \Omega$ alone; branch 2 is $R_2=4\ \Omega$ in series with $R_3=2\ \Omega$; branch 3 is $R_4=3\ \Omega$ in series with $R_5=3\ \Omega$. Find $R_{eq}$.
80. Nested reduction: $\{[R_1=8\ \Omega \parallel R_2=8\ \Omega] \text{ series } R_3=4\ \Omega\} \parallel \{[R_4=6\ \Omega \text{ series } R_5=6\ \Omega] \parallel R_6=12\ \Omega\}$. Find $R_{eq}$.
81. Given that a series/parallel reduction of some network of six resistors gives $R_{eq}=40\ \Omega$, and five of the six resistors are given as $10,10,10,10,10\ \Omega$ (wired: first four in parallel, that result in series with the fifth), find the value of the sixth resistor if it's placed in series with everything found so far.
82. A network has $R_1=1\ \Omega$ through $R_5=5\ \Omega$ (five different values, $1,2,3,4,5\ \Omega$) all wired in series in some order. Find $R_{eq}$ and explain why the *order* the resistors are wired in series never affects the total.
83. The same five resistors ($1,2,3,4,5\ \Omega$) are instead all wired in parallel with each other. Find $R_{eq}$ (exact fraction).
84. A resistor bridge network (Wheatstone-style): $R_1=100\ \Omega$ (node $A$–$B$), $R_2=200\ \Omega$ (node $A$–$C$), $R_3=200\ \Omega$ (node $B$–$D$), $R_4=400\ \Omega$ (node $C$–$D$), and a bridge resistor $R_5=150\ \Omega$ (node $B$–$C$). Explain specifically why you cannot find $R_{eq}$ (from $A$ to $D$) using only series/parallel reduction, identifying which node(s) violate the structural requirements — do not attempt to force a numeric answer.
85. In the same bridge network as Problem 84, suppose instead $R_5$ (the bridge resistor) is removed entirely (open circuit, no connection between $B$ and $C$). Now find $R_{eq}$ from $A$ to $D$ using ordinary series/parallel reduction, and explain what specifically changed structurally to make this now possible.
86. In the same original bridge network as Problem 84, suppose instead $R_5$ is replaced with a plain wire (short circuit, $R=0\ \Omega$, directly connecting $B$ and $C$). Nodes $B$ and $C$ are now actually the same node. Redraw the network in your head with $B$ and $C$ merged, and find $R_{eq}$ from $A$ to $D$ (this case IS reducible by series/parallel — explain why merging $B,C$ restores the needed structure).
87. A network has seven resistors of value $R$ each (same $R$, unspecified), wired as: three in series, that whole chain in parallel with the remaining four (which are themselves all in parallel with each other). Express $R_{eq}$ as a multiple of $R$.
88. Two separate resistor networks, each with its own $R_{eq}$ already known to be $20\ \Omega$ and $30\ \Omega$ respectively, are then wired in parallel with each other (treating each network as a single black-box resistor at its already-known $R_{eq}$). Find the combined $R_{eq}$.
89. The same two networks (20Ω-equivalent and 30Ω-equivalent) are instead wired in series with each other. Find the combined $R_{eq}$.
90. A network has $R_1=1\ \text{k}\Omega$ in parallel with $R_2=1\ \text{k}\Omega$ in parallel with $R_3=1\ \text{k}\Omega$ in parallel with $R_4=1\ \text{k}\Omega$ — four equal resistors, all parallel. Find $R_{eq}$ and state the general pattern for $n$ equal resistors $R$ in parallel.
91. A resistor network has an outer resistor $R_1=20\ \Omega$ directly across the two terminals, in parallel with an inner branch consisting of $R_2=5\ \Omega$ in series with [$R_3=10\ \Omega$ parallel with $R_4=10\ \Omega$]. Find $R_{eq}$.
92. A "double ladder": $R_1=4\ \Omega$ series, then a rung $R_2=8\ \Omega$ to the return path, then $R_3=4\ \Omega$ series, then a rung $R_4=8\ \Omega$, then $R_5=4\ \Omega}$ series, terminated by a final rung $R_6=8\ \Omega$. Reduce from the far end and find $R_{eq}$.
93. A network claimed by a classmate to have $R_{eq}=15\ \Omega$ consists of $R_1=10\ \Omega$ in series with $R_2=20\ \Omega$, that pair in parallel with a third resistor $R_3$ of unknown value. Determine what value $R_3$ would have to be for the classmate's claim to be correct (or show no positive real value of $R_3$ achieves it, if that's the case).
94. Six identical resistors, each $R=30\ \Omega$, form the edges of a network where three are in series to form one path, and the other three are in series to form a second, parallel path between the same two terminals. Find $R_{eq}$.
95. A network has $R_1=9\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_2=18\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_3=6\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_4=36\ \Omega$ — four resistors, all parallel, no nesting. Find $R_{eq}$ (exact fraction).
96. Explain, in general terms (not for a specific numeric network), why a series/parallel-reducible network's $R_{eq}$ is always uniquely determined regardless of what order you choose to do the reductions in — i.e. why doing the "innermost" combination first versus doing an "outer" one first (when both are legally reducible at that stage) can never lead to two different final answers.
97. A resistor divider network has $R_1=3\ \text{k}\Omega$ in series with $R_2=3\ \text{k}\Omega$ in series with $R_3=3\ \text{k}\Omega}$; additionally, a fourth resistor $R_4=9\ \text{k}\Omega$ connects directly across the outer two terminals (in parallel with the entire three-resistor series chain). 98. A network has ten resistors, all $R=100\ \Omega$, wired as five parallel pairs (each pair being two $100\ \Omega$ resistors in parallel, giving five results of $50\ \Omega$ each), and those five $50\ \Omega$ results are then wired in series with each other. Find the overall $R_{eq}$.
99. A bridge-like network has $R_1=50\ \Omega$ ($A$–$B$), $R_2=50\ \Omega$ ($A$–$C$), $R_3=50\ \Omega$ ($B$–$D$), $R_4=50\ \Omega$ ($C$–$D$), and $R_5=50\ \Omega$ (the bridge, $B$–$C$) — all five equal. 100. A network has twelve identical resistors, each $R=12\ \Omega$, arranged as four groups of three, where each group of three is wired in series, and the four resulting series groups are then wired in parallel with each other. Find the overall $R_{eq}$, then generalize: if there were $m$ groups of $n$ series resistors each (all value $R$) wired in parallel, express $R_{eq}$ in terms of $m$, $n$, and $R$.
