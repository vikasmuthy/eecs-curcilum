# Drill Bank — Thevenin/Norton Equivalents

100 worded problems for 6.002 Topic 03's Thevenin/Norton material: finding $v_{th}$ (=$v_{oc}$), $R_{th}$ (via zeroed-source resistance, the test-current method, or $v_{oc}/i_{sc}$), Norton conversion ($i_{no}=v_{th}/R_{th}$, $R_{no}=R_{th}$), and using the terminal law $v=v_{th}-R_{th}i$ once a load is attached. No solutions, no hints, no worked steps.

**Self-verification method for this topic:** once you have $v_{th}$ and $R_{th}$, check them two independent ways. (1) Compute $i_{sc}$ directly (short the terminals, solve the resulting circuit) and confirm $R_{th}=v_{oc}/i_{sc}$ matches what you got from the zeroed-source (or test-current) method — these are two different derivations of the same number, so they must agree. (2) If a load is given, verify the terminal law $v=v_{th}-R_{th}i$ against a *direct* node-analysis solve of the original network with that load attached — both must produce the same load voltage/current. If either check fails, don't just recompute the same way again — the whole point is that two genuinely different methods must land on the same answer.

Scope note: every problem is solvable using $v_{oc}$ (open-circuit node analysis), $R_{th}$ (zeroed independent sources, resistor combination when valid or the test-current method when a dependent source's controlling loop is internal to the port), $i_{sc}$, and the terminal law — nothing beyond what this note establishes.

## Batch 1 (1–25) — Independent sources and resistors only (no dependent sources)

3. A one-port has a $2\text{ A}$ independent current source in parallel with $R_1=10\ \Omega$, both connected directly across the two output terminals. Find $v_{th}$ and $R_{th}$.
4. A one-port has a $5\text{ A}$ current source in parallel with two resistors, $R_1=20\ \Omega$ and $R_2=20\ \Omega$ (all three elements sharing the same two terminals). Find $v_{th}$ and $R_{th}$.
5. A one-port has a $10\text{ V}$ source in series with $R_1=2\ \Omega$, feeding a node that also connects to the output terminals through $R_2=8\ \Omega$ in parallel with $R_3=8\ \Omega$ (both from that node to the far terminal). Find $v_{th}$ and $R_{th}$.

8. A one-port has two independent voltage sources in series with two resistors: $8\text{ V}$–$R_1=2\ \Omega$–$4\text{ V}$ (opposing polarity)–$R_2=2\ \Omega$, forming the whole one-port between the two end terminals. Find $v_{th}$ and $R_{th}$.

14. A one-port has a $30\text{ V}$ source through $R_1=10\ \Omega$ into node $X$; node $X$ connects to terminal $A$ through $R_2=5\ \Omega$ and to terminal $B$ through $R_3=15\ \Omega$. Find $v_{th}$ and $R_{th}$ across $A$–$B$.

17. A one-port has a $1\text{ A}$ current source in series with $R_1=1000\ \Omega$ — note that a current source in *series* with a resistor is unusual; explain why the resistor value doesn't affect $i_{sc}$ or $R_{th}$ in this configuration, then find $v_{th}$, $R_{th}$.

## Batch 3 (51–75) — Load attachment (terminal law) and real-world framing
